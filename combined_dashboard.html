This Week's Findings

WIN

Sun Prairie snapped back from -19.6% to +1.8% — the conversion gap finally closed
For three straight weeks this was the deepest decline in the company (-19.2%, then -19.6%) even though search kept saying demand was there — it still ranks position 2.2, the best of any location, with 405 organic clicks and a healthy 3.90 ROAS. This week sales caught up to the search signal and turned positive, a ~21-point swing with spend held flat at $556 (0.47% of sales). This was the textbook 'people find it but don't buy' pattern, and it resolved on the operator side, not through added budget. Worth a quick confirm with the GM on what changed (hours, staffing, service speed) so it sticks.

WATCH

MKE Public Market recovered to -0.5% but still spends the most for the least — hold, don't add
Last week's top concern improved on the axis that matters: net sales climbed from -10.2% to nearly flat (-0.5%) and ROAS rose from 2.36 to 2.70. But the efficiency flags are all still lit — spend climbed again to $1,127 (up +$114, the highest of any location), ROAS is still the lowest in the company, and ad spend is 1.40% of sales versus ~0.5% at the efficient stores. Organic is steady at 251 clicks, so visibility isn't the issue. The sales recovery justifies not cutting hard, but the same dollars still return ~1.6x more at Whitefish Bay (4.53) or Brookfield (4.34). Action: hold spend flat, don't feed it further, and re-check ROAS next week before deciding whether the recovery is real or seasonal.

CONCERN

Champaign is now the worst decliner at -7.7% with sales AND search both falling — real demand loss
This is the one location where the two channels agree in the bad direction: net sales are down -7.7% (from $43,984 to $40,615, the smallest-revenue store) and organic clicks fell -15% (286 to 242). When sales and search decline together it's a genuine demand problem on the ground, not a marketing gap — adding ad budget won't fix it, and spend was already cut to $413. The tell is the ratio: at $40.6k in sales, that $413 is 1.02% of sales, high for a small store. Action: this needs an operator look (local competition, foot traffic, staffing) rather than a budget move.

WIN

Middleton and Pewaukee prove the lean-spend pattern: -0.2% and +7.8% while budgets came down
The two efficiency models both improved again. Middleton is essentially flat (-0.2%, up from -4.1%) on the leanest spend in the company — $558, just 0.42% of sales — with ROAS up to 3.90 and organic steady at 160 clicks. Pewaukee is now the strongest grower at +7.8% (from -0.1%) with spend trimmed to $838. In both cases sales rose as spend fell, which is efficiency improving, not sales being bought. One caution on Pewaukee: its ROAS slipped from 4.21 to 3.53 and ad spend is 1.19% of sales, so the growth is getting more expensive per dollar — watch whether that ratio keeps climbing.

WATCH

Whitefish Bay still under-fed: highest ROAS (4.53) and strongest organic, only 0.46% of sales
Sales improved from -7.1% to -4.5%, but this store remains the clearest reallocation target. It has the highest ROAS in the company (4.53), by far the strongest organic (604 clicks, up from 578), and yet gets only $557 — 0.46% of sales — while MKE spends $1,127 at a 2.70 ROAS. Every ad dollar works hardest here and demand is plainly there. Action: test shifting a slice of the MKE budget to Whitefish Bay and watch whether the remaining sales softness closes at a still-excellent ROAS. This is the same MKE-to-WFB reallocation flagged last week; the case for it is stronger now that MKE has stabilized.
