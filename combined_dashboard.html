This Week's Findings

CONCERN

MKE Public Market: spend rose +$114 while sales fell to -10.2% — budget moving the wrong way
This was the top concern last week and it worsened on every axis. Spend went UP from $1,013 to $1,127 (now the highest of any location) while net sales slid from -6.5% to -10.2%. ROAS is still the lowest in the company at 2.70, and ad spend is 1.43% of sales versus ~0.5% at the efficient stores. Organic is flat (250 clicks), so this isn't a visibility gap. Last week's recommendation to cut spend was not acted on; instead it climbed and sales fell further. The same dollars return ~1.7x more at Whitefish Bay (4.53) or Brookfield (4.34). Action: cut MKE back toward 0.5-0.7% of sales and stop feeding a channel that isn't reaching the register.

CONCERN

Sun Prairie down -19.6%, still the worst in the company while its search is the strongest
A persistent concern, essentially unchanged from -19.2% last week. Search continues to say demand is there: organic clicks rose again to 460 (+8%) and its page ranks position 2.2, the best of any location, with a healthy 3.90 ROAS. People keep finding Sun Prairie and not buying — a conversion or on-the-ground problem, not a marketing one. Spend has already been cut to $556 (from $856), so adding budget won't fix it. This has now been the deepest decline for three weeks running and needs an operator look: hours, staffing, service speed, a new competitor, or a local disruption.

WATCH

Whitefish Bay -7.1% and still sliding after another spend cut — over-cut thesis holding
Last week's watch — that the spend cut had gone too deep — is playing out. Spend was pulled again to $557 (from $898) and sales worsened from -5.6% to -7.1%, yet this store has the highest ROAS in the company (4.53) and by far the strongest organic (595 clicks, up +21). Every remaining ad dollar works hard and demand is clearly there; the store is being under-fed at 0.46% of sales. This is the exact opposite of MKE. Action: shift a slice of the MKE budget here and test whether the lost sales come back at a still-excellent ROAS.

WATCH

Brookfield flipped from +7.0% to -3.6% and now carries the highest ad-spend ratio (1.60%)
Last week's standout winner cooled off. Sales went from +7.0% to -3.6%, ROAS eased from 4.71 to 4.34 (still second-best), and spend was trimmed to $971. The flag is the ratio: ad spend is now 1.60% of sales, the highest of any location, on a store that had been growing on its own momentum. Organic is steady (344 clicks). Not a problem yet — the ROAS is strong — but worth watching whether the new location's early ramp is leveling into normal seasonal softness, and whether a 1.6% spend ratio is still justified once growth stops paying for itself.

WIN

Middleton and Pewaukee: sales nearly flat on lean ad spend — the efficient pattern
Two bright spots against a company-wide down period (all ten stores are negative vs the prior four weeks). Middleton cut spend in half ($1,090 to $558, now the leanest at 0.42% of sales), yet organic clicks rose to 177 (+20%), ROAS climbed to 3.90, and sales held at -4.1%. Pewaukee continued its recovery — from -10.3% two weeks ago to -3.7% last week to essentially flat now (-0.1%). Both show sales holding as spend comes down: efficiency improving, not sales being bought. These are the models for disciplined budgets and the template for the MKE-to-Whitefish-Bay reallocation.
