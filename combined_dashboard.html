This Week's Findings

WIN

Sun Prairie completed its turnaround: -19.6% to +3.5%, the biggest swing in the company
For three straight weeks this was the deepest decline in the company (around -19%) even though search kept saying demand was there — it still ranks position 2.2, the best of any location, with 405 organic clicks and a strong 4.28 ROAS. Sales have now fully caught up to the search signal and turned solidly positive (+3.5%), a ~23-point swing, with spend actually trimmed to $526 (0.44% of sales). This was the textbook 'people find it but don't buy' pattern and it resolved on the operator side, not through added budget. Worth confirming with the GM what changed (hours, staffing, service speed) so the fix holds.

WIN

MKE Public Market reversed last week's top concern: spend cut $209, sales flipped to +3.6%
This was the #1 concern for two weeks — spend rising while sales fell to -10.2%. That has now cleanly reversed: spend was cut from $1,131 to $922 (-$209) and net sales flipped from -10.2% to +3.6%, with ROAS ticking up from 2.61 to 2.83. The recommended budget discipline was applied and sales recovered rather than falling further, which says the earlier spend was not what was driving the register. One caveat to stay disciplined on: MKE still runs the lowest ROAS in the company (2.83) and the second-highest ad ratio (1.11% of sales), so hold the line here — don't let spend drift back up now that sales have stabilized.

CONCERN

Champaign worsened to -9.8% with sales AND search both falling — the clearest demand problem
Against a week where most stores recovered, Champaign went the other way — from -7.7% to -9.8%, now the worst decline in the company. It's the one location where both channels agree in the bad direction: net sales down -9.8% (to $40,120, the smallest-revenue store) and organic clicks down -15% (286 to 242). When sales and search fall together it's a genuine on-the-ground demand problem, not a marketing gap — adding ad budget won't fix it, and spend was already trimmed to $363. Action: this needs an operator look (local competition, foot traffic, staffing, campus seasonality) rather than a budget move.

WATCH

ROAS eroding at the high-spend stores: Brookfield 5.22 to 3.81, Hilldale 4.93 to 3.44
With fresh ads numbers in, two of the bigger-spending locations show efficiency slipping fast. Brookfield's ROAS dropped from 5.22 to 3.81 while it still carries the highest ad ratio in the company (1.42% of sales) and sales sit at -3.3% — the new-location ramp appears to be leveling into normal softness, and the spend ratio is getting harder to justify. Hilldale fell from 4.93 to 3.44 with sales at -5.7%. Neither is a crisis (both still profitable), but the direction is the opposite of the lean stores, and both are spending well above the ~0.44% that the efficient locations run. Action: watch one more week; if ROAS keeps sliding, trim toward the efficient benchmark.

WIN

Lean-spend pattern still wins: Pewaukee +9.2% and Middleton -0.5% on cut budgets
The efficiency models led the recovery. Pewaukee is the strongest grower in the company at +9.2% (up from around -0.1%) with spend trimmed to $737, and Middleton is essentially flat (-0.5%) on the leanest spend anywhere — $531, just 0.40% of sales — with ROAS up to 4.26. In both cases sales rose or held as spend came down: efficiency improving, not sales being bought. The same setup still points at Whitefish Bay as under-fed — it has the highest ROAS in the company (4.59) and by far the strongest organic (604 clicks) yet gets only 0.44% of sales — so it remains the best home for any dollars freed up by the Brookfield/Hilldale trims.
